This page lists some resources related to competitive programming: online judges, problem collections, books, tools and more.

## Online judges

An online judge (Online Judging System, OJ for short) is usually used for practicing problems, taking part in and organizing contests, and sharing and discussing with other users.

### In China

-   [51Nod](https://www.51nod.com/): has many math and thinking problems worth trying.
-   [Comet OJ](https://www.cometoj.com): started in 2018. It aims to give algorithm enthusiasts a place to compete, practice and communicate. It often holds high-quality contests with original problems and has a rich problemset.
-   [HDU Online Judge](http://acm.hdu.edu.cn/): started in 2005. The online judge of Hangzhou Dianzi University, with problems from the Multi-University Training Contests.
-   [HydroOJ](https://hydro.ac/): started in 2021. The official site of the open-source project [Hydro](https://hydro.js.org/). Users can create their own [domains](https://hydro.ac/discuss/6087cc44e098b0cd7dde1a0c), which can use the same features as the main site, such as problemsets, contests and discussions.
-   [Judge Duck Online](https://duck.ac/): based on the open-source project [JudgeDuck](https://github.com/JudgeDuck) developed by [wangyisong1996](https://github.com/wangyisong1996). It can measure a program's running time to the microsecond. (It has few problems.)
-   [LibreOJ](https://loj.ac/): started in 2017. Based on the open-source project [Lyrio](https://github.com/lyrio-dev/lyrio); "Libre" means free. All test data and submitted code are open to all users. It is currently maintained by [Menci](https://github.com/Menci).
-   [CDOJ](https://cdoj.site/d/lutece/): the online judge of the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, started in 2012.
-   [Luogu](https://www.luogu.com.cn/): started in 2013. It has a huge community and a fairly complete collection of past OI problems and exercises. It also offers paid educational services.
-   [Nowcoder](https://www.nowcoder.com/): started in 2014. It offers services for tech job preparation, community discussion, company recruiting and more.
-   [OpenJudge](http://openjudge.cn/): started in 2005. A group judging platform developed by the POJ team.
-   [POJ](http://poj.org/): the online judge of Peking University, started in 2003, one of the oldest OJs in China. It has many problems in English, including both basic problems and good problems worth trying. POJ problems can be submitted in the [Bailian](http://bailian.openjudge.cn/practice/) problemset.
-   [PTA (Pintia)](https://pintia.cn/): started in 2016. A product of Hangzhou Baiteng Education Technology Co., Ltd., a spin-off of Zhejiang University.
-   [QOJ](https://qoj.ac/): collects many OI and ICPC problems from China and abroad, useful for training.
-   [Universal Online Judge](https://uoj.ac/): started in 2014; "Universal" means general-purpose. The [project is open source](https://github.com/UniversalOJ/UOJ-System). It is [VFK](https://github.com/vfleaking)'s OJ, with many original contest problems and CCF/THU problems, and fairly high difficulty.
-   [Vijos](https://vijos.org/): started in 2005. Projects such as the [server](https://github.com/vijos/vj4) and the [judge](https://github.com/vijos/jd4) are open source.
-   [WZOI](https://wzoi.cn): started in 2017. An [open-source](https://github.com/massimodong/wzoj) judging system maintained by Wenzhou High School in Zhejiang Province.
-   [ZOJ](https://zoj.pintia.cn/home): the online judge of Zhejiang University, started in 2001.

### Outside China

-   [AizuOJ](https://onlinejudge.u-aizu.ac.jp): the online judge of the University of Aizu in Japan, started in 2004. It includes problems from several Japanese high school and university programming contests, and comes with introductory courses on programming, data structures and algorithms.
-   [AtCoder](https://atcoder.jp/): a Japanese OJ. The Japanese version also has contests from Japanese universities that do not show up in the English version. The problems are interesting and of high quality.
-   [CodeChef](https://codechef.com/): an Indian OJ that holds contests regularly. Its system is based on SPOJ's Sphere Engine.
-   [Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/): a Russian OJ, started in 2010 by [Mike Mirzayanov](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mike-mirzayanov-31772a93/). It has several series of contests and lets individuals set problems and apply to organize contests. The problems are of high quality.
-   [CSES](https://cses.fi/problemset/) (Code Submission Evaluation System): a problemset organized by topic that [aims](https://cses.fi/problemset/text/2433) to be a comprehensive, high-quality problemset. It is mainly developed by Antti Laaksonen, author of the [Competitive Programmer's Handbook](https://cses.fi/book/book.pdf), and started in 2013.
-   [CS Academy](https://csacademy.com/)
-   [DMOJ](https://dmoj.ca/): an open-source Canadian OJ with support for many languages. Its problemset archives many major contests, and it also holds its own contests regularly.
-   [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/): has many contests.
-   [Kattis](https://open.kattis.com/): the problemset mostly contains ICPC-style problems. It rates users based on what they have solved and recommends trivial/easy/medium/hard problems suited to their level. Problem difficulty is rated with an [Elo rating](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%AD%89%E7%BA%A7%E5%88%86)-like system.
-   [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/): a site for practicing coding-interview problems, with a Chinese branch: [LeetCode China](https://leetcode.cn).
-   [Light OJ](https://lightoj.com)
-   [Open Trains](https://opentrains.opencup.org/): the training platform of the Russian Open Cup, built on the open-source [ejudge](https://ejudge.ru/) system, with support for virtual contests. Its problemset includes past Open Cup problems and problems from the Petrozavodsk training camp.
-   [SPOJ](http://www.spoj.com): started in 2003. Its backend system [Sphere Engine](https://sphere-engine.com/) became a commercial product in 2008. It supports upvoting and tagging problems.
-   [TopCoder](https://www.topcoder.com/): started in 2001. Its [competitive programming community](https://www.topcoder.com/community/competitive-programming/) has many contests. Its main business today is tech crowdsourcing.
-   [TimusOJ](http://acm.timus.ru/): started in 2000 and developed by Ural Federal University. It has the largest online judge problemset in Russia. Its problems mainly come from Ural Federal University contests, the Ural Championship, the ICPC Ural Regional, and the Petrozavodsk training camp.
-   Online Judge (formerly [UVaOJ](https://uva.onlinejudge.org/)): started in 1995, the earliest OJ to become internationally famous. It was founded by Professor Miguel Ángel Revilla of the University of Valladolid (UVa) in Spain. Since [Professor Revilla sadly passed away in 2018](https://www.elnortedecastilla.es/valladolid/muere-profesor-miguel-20180402225739-nt.html) and the University of Valladolid stopped maintaining it, UVaOJ was renamed Online Judge in July 2019. Its current maintainers [are building a new judging platform on GitHub](https://github.com/TheOnlineJudge/ojudge).
-   [Yandex](https://contest.yandex.ru/): archives the All-Russian Olympiad in Informatics of recent years.

## Tools

-   [*100 gdb Tips*](https://github.com/hellogcc/100-gdb-tips)
-   [Algorithm Visualizer](http://algorithm-visualizer.org)
-   [cppreference](https://zh.cppreference.com/w/): a comprehensive online reference for the C and C++ languages and their standard libraries
-   [Compiler Explorer](https://godbolt.org): view the assembly generated for your code online, with a choice of compilers
-   [C++ Insights](https://cppinsights.io/): see your C++ source code through the compiler's eyes
-   [Inverse Symbolic Calculator](http://wayback.cecm.sfu.ca/projects/ISC/ISCmain.html): look up an expression from a real number, useful for figuring out where a constant comes from
-   [$\rm\LaTeX$ handwritten symbol recognition](http://detexify.kirelabs.org/classify.html)
-   [$\rm\LaTeX$ math symbol reference](http://www.mohu.org/info/symbols/symbols.htm)
-   [Mathpix](https://mathpix.com/): turn screenshots into $\rm\LaTeX{}$
-   [OEIS](https://oeis.org): a search engine for integer sequences
-   [Python Tutor](https://pythontutor.com/): visualize how code runs step by step
-   [Quick C++ Benchmark](https://quick-bench.com/): compare the running speed of two or more functions online
-   [Try It Online](https://tio.run): run code in 600+ languages online, with I/O support, a 60 s timeout and code sharing
-   [Graph editor](https://csacademy.com/app/graph_editor/) and [GraphViz](http://www.graphviz.org/)
-   [uDebug](https://www.udebug.com): debugging help for some OJ problems
-   [USF](https://www.cs.usfca.edu/~galles/visualization/) and [VisuAlgo](https://visualgo.net/zh): algorithm visualization
-   [Wandbox](https://wandbox.org/): run code online in 30+ languages, with code sharing and a choice of compiler versions
-   [Wolfram Alpha](https://www.wolframalpha.com/): computes answers to questions on many topics, including math, science and technology, society and culture, and more
